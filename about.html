About Acting Through Interactivity

This site is to discuss and explore the topic of narrative and games. While this may feel limited to just story-based games, this can actually extend to defining characters and the setting of a game. For example, in a horror game, risk added to losing to make encounters riskier and scarier. Or enemy design to make fights more personal. These aspects of games are somewhat ignored when separating story and gameplay as separated ratings, when they often work together.

Why video games?

Video games are just like any other piece of media in that they can make you think and immerse yourself in new perspectives. However, while books can make you imagine a story, a movie can make you watch a story, a video game lets you participate in a story. Games put you into a role and let you act out within it while letting the systems lead you into your role. Or in other cases, they let you define your role fully and separate a story out for you specifically. This is what makes the medium unique compared to others and the interactive space can even blur the lines between movie or TV series, with the key feature is that it is interactive.

Should you have hours of experience before being able to talk about interactivity in narratives?

No. I believe that there is value in different perspectives and just because some aspects are considered the standard for some, it does not mean that it should be acceptable for everyone. There is also value in what someone new to video games feels, because certain aspects feel standard while also being someone else's first introduction to it. You don't need to have beaten every Souls game or have hundreds of hours logged in several games, because the main focus of this site is narrative, and all people have experience that can make judgments on narratives.
